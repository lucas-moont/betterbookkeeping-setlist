# Completing a workout with no sets: what other apps do (2026)

Research note, written 2026-11-05. Scope: what Setlist should do when a user marks a Current Workout complete and it has no Sets. The options are refuse, allow, discard, or a variant of these. The note covers how established strength-logging and activity apps handle empty or very short sessions, what UX guidance says about destructive defaults, and whether any app keeps attendance separate from logged training. Bracketed numbers point to [Sources](#6-sources). Text marked **(analysis)** is my own reasoning, not a sourced claim.

## 1. TL;DR

- **No surveyed strength log documents keeping an empty workout as a history record (option b).** The two apps that document the case both prevent the record. In Hevy, a session can be finished "so long as you mark at least one set as complete" [1] (a vendor FAQ on a feature page, not the help centre). StrongLifts says "A workout with no logged sets is not saved" [13].
- **FitNotes avoids the problem by design.** It has no session or "finish" entity. A workout is the sets logged on a date, so an empty workout has nothing to show **(analysis, from [18][19])**.
- **Strong, JEFIT and Fitbod: not found.** Their help centres don't say what happens on finishing with zero sets. Strong says only that you can finish "at any time" and the workout is "safely recorded" [8].
- **Activity apps use a duration threshold and an explicit Discard.** Strava's Discard asks for confirmation and "there is no way for Strava to recover the activity" [25]. Strava streaks count any activity of 60 s or more, including manual ones [26].
- **Attendance is tracked separately only in gym-membership tools** (check-in counts [30][31]) and in StrengthLog's "Manual check-off" streak type [29]. Workout loggers count streaks from saved workouts [4][16][29].
- **UX guidance:** never discard silently. Confirm only uncommon actions that can't be undone, and prefer undo otherwise [32][37]. Apple's HIG uses the Mail draft as its example: when you cancel, an action sheet offers delete, save, or keep editing [37]. That is the same decision Setlist faces.
- **Recommendation for Setlist (analysis):** when the workout is empty, Complete opens a choice of *Save as gym visit*, *Discard workout* or *Keep logging*, and none of them is the default. A saved empty workout appears in history as "No sets logged", not "0 sets", and is left out of any training count. This meets the product owner's attendance requirement without destroying data silently. Charts are unaffected because they only use Sets.

**Pattern (analysis):** strength logs define a valid workout by its content (at least one set) [1][13]. Activity trackers define it by a duration or distance threshold [24][26][27]. None of the apps surveyed keeps a deliberately empty session as a record. For *partly* finished workouts, StrengthLog asks on save whether to delete the unfinished sets or move them to a planned workout [28]. Where discarding is possible, it is an explicit user action, and Strava confirms it [2][25].

## 3. UX principles that apply

1. **Prevent the error, through constraints and good defaults.** NN/g heuristic #5: "the best designs carefully prevent problems from occurring in the first place. Either eliminate error-prone conditions, or check for them and present users with a confirmation option before they commit." Tip: "Avoid slips by providing helpful constraints and good defaults" [33]. Also: "start by offering reasonable defaults that are likely to fit their real-world goals" [35]. Hevy's at-least-one-set gate is a constraint of this kind [1].
2. **Confirm only uncommon actions that can't be undone.** Apple HIG: "Avoid displaying alerts for common, undoable actions, even when they're destructive… when people take an uncommon destructive action that they can't undo, it's important to display an alert" [37]. NN/g: "Do not use confirmation dialogs for routine actions"; "if you cry wolf too many times, people will stop paying attention" [32]. Material (M2): confirmation isn't necessary when the consequences of an action are reversible or negligible, and acknowledgements "may include an option to undo" [38].
3. **Undo is the stronger safety net.** NN/g: "do go to great lengths to provide undo" [32]. Heuristic #3 (user control and freedom): "Support Undo and Redo" and give a clearly labelled "emergency exit" [33]. "Don't use confirmation dialogs as the sole error prevention method" [34].
4. **When an intentional action has several outcomes, offer them as choices.** Apple HIG, iOS: "Use an action sheet — not an alert — to offer choices related to an intentional action. For example, when people cancel the Mail message they're editing, an action sheet provides three choices: delete the edits (or the entire draft), save the draft, or return to editing" [37]. An empty Complete has the same three outcomes: discard, keep, or go back and log sets **(analysis)**.
5. **Make confirmations specific, and avoid a destructive default.** NN/g: explain what will happen instead of asking "Are you sure?". Label buttons by their outcome, and "it's probably best not to have a default answer at all" [32]. HIG: use button titles like "Delete" rather than "OK", always include "Cancel" when the action is destructive, and avoid a default button if you want people to read the alert [37].
6. **An empty state should still communicate status and give a way forward.** NN/g: "Totally empty states cause confusion about how and whether the system is working". Use them to "provide direct pathways… to getting started with key tasks" [36]. In Setlist, a history row reading "0 sets" looks like a bug. "No sets logged" with a route to add sets or delete the row says what the row is **(analysis)**.

Implication (analysis): silent discard (option c with no feedback) breaks principles 2–3, because it destroys data without confirmation or undo. A plain block (option a) is a valid constraint (principle 1), but on its own it leaves no exit for "I'm done and did nothing". Hevy pairs its block with a Discard button [1][2].

## 4. Attendance vs. training data

- **Workout loggers derive streaks and consistency from saved workouts, not from check-ins.** Hevy's streak is "how many weeks in a row you have completed a workout" [4][6]. Fitbod counts "workouts logged during the week" [16]. StrengthLog's workout streaks are "checked off automatically once you save a workout that contains the goal of the streak" [29]. In each case an empty session can't count, because it is never saved [1][13] **(analysis for Fitbod and StrengthLog, whose behaviour with empty workouts is undocumented)**.
- **Hevy treats any logged activity as attendance in practice.** Its streak-recovery article says that if you didn't work out you "can log a walk or any other activity" in that week [5]. The streak measures showing up, but it still needs a non-empty logged item **(analysis)**.
- **StrengthLog models attendance explicitly.** It offers a *Manual check-off* streak type ("streaks that you need to check off by yourself") next to a *Workout logging* type [29]. This is the closest documented example of separating "I showed up" from logged training in a lifting app.
- **Gym-membership software counts check-ins, not sets.** PushPress's member app tracks "check-in totals and qualifying activities (e.g., classes, appointments, open gym)", and progress "updates automatically with each check-in" [30]. Planet Fitness's app tracks "club check ins and average workout duration" [31] (press release, read through a search snippet because the page returned 403).
- **Activity platforms use time as the attendance floor.** Strava counts any activity of 60 s or more, including manual entries [26]. Apple's Exercise ring counts brisk minutes whether or not a workout was started [22].

**For Setlist (analysis):** the product owner's case ("went to the gym and did nothing") is attendance. The industry tracks attendance either as a check-in record that is separate from training (gym software, StrengthLog manual streaks) or with a minimum duration (Strava). Once `startedAt` lands, a completed workout with no sets carries real information: when the user was at the gym and for how long. That makes it a reasonable carrier for a visit record. It needs to be labelled as a visit and not counted as training.

## 5. Options for Setlist

Setlist's progression charts plot one point per training day from Sets only, so **an empty workout never produces a chart point** under any option. What changes between options is the history table, any future count-based stats ("workouts this week", streaks), and how the Complete flow behaves.

| Option | Behaviour | Pros | Cons | Precedent |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **(a) Refuse** | Complete is disabled or returns an error while there are no sets. | Simple invariant: every completed workout has at least one set, so history and stats stay clean. Matches Hevy [1]. | It contradicts the product owner's view. With no exit, the user is stuck with an active workout, and the one-active-workout rule blocks starting the next one. It needs a separate Discard action (as Hevy has [2]). | Hevy [1] |
| **(b) Allow** (current) | The empty workout is saved and shown as "0 sets". | Matches the product owner's attendance view. No data is destroyed. Charts are unaffected. | No surveyed strength log does this. "0 sets" reads like a bug [36]. Accidental starts pile up as junk rows. Future workout counts or streaks would count non-training, unless each such stat filters it out. | None found among loggers. Strava saves short activities but needs 60 s to count them [26]. |
| **(c) Discard** | Completing an empty workout deletes it. | Clean history. StrongLifts does exactly this [13]. | Silent destruction of a user action, against [32][33][37]. It contradicts the product owner's view. A user who meant "I went" gets no feedback and no record. | StrongLifts [13]; Zwift below its threshold [27] |
| **Confirm-then-discard** | Complete with no sets opens a dialog: "This workout has no sets. Discard it?" with *Discard* / *Keep logging*. | Not silent. The user decides. The rare-and-irreversible case is exactly where HIG and NN/g say to confirm [32][37]. | Still offers no way to keep the visit, so it contradicts the product owner's view. | Strava's Discard confirmation [25]; StrongLifts' finish prompt [13] |
| **Allow-but-exclude-from-stats** | The empty workout is saved, rendered distinctly ("No sets logged" or "Gym visit"), and left out of training counts. | Matches the product owner's view. History reads correctly. Charts are already safe. It becomes richer once `startedAt` provides a duration. | Every future stat must decide whether it counts visits. A rule needs writing down: "a training day is a day with at least one Set". Accidental empty workouts still accumulate unless there is a prompt. | StrengthLog manual check-off [29]; gym check-ins [30][31] |
| **Recommended hybrid (analysis)** | On Complete with no sets, show an action sheet: **Save as gym visit** / **Discard workout** / **Keep logging**, with no default button [32][37]. A saved visit behaves as in the row above, and deleting it from history is available (ideally with undo) [32][33]. | It covers both the attendance case and the accidental start, and it is never silent. It follows the HIG Mail-draft pattern directly [37]. The prompt appears only for empty workouts, which are rare, so it won't cause confirmation fatigue [32]. | It costs one extra step on an uncommon path. History rendering needs a variant, and the "training day" definition must be explicit in the glossary or ADRs. | Composite of [2][25][29][37] |

**Decision points for the product owner (analysis):**
1. Is an empty completed workout a *visit* (kept, labelled) or *noise* (discarded)? The product owner's stated view points to visit. The hybrid lets the user decide each time.
2. If visits are kept, does anything other than history count them (a future streak or a "workouts this week" figure)? Industry streaks are content-based [4][16] or duration-based [26]. Neither is set-free and duration-free.
3. Should a minimum duration from `startedAt` (as with Strava's 60 s [26]) mark a two-second accidental start as discard-suggested? This is optional. Don't ship it without confirming the start-time semantics.
